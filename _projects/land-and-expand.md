I'm very interested in how AI can augment the reading and [writing](/projects/half-formed-thought) process. There are many books I've given up on because they were too dense and convoluted for my feeble brain (looking at you Heidegger) or required a lot of background reading just to make sense of it.

[Land And Expand](https://dmvaldman.github.io/LandAndExpand/) was an experiment in reading Nick Land's essay [Meltdown](https://web.archive.org/web/20250201021520/https://www.ccru.net/swarm1/1_melt.htm) with the help of Gemini 2.5 Pro.

My process was one long-lived [chat](https://gemini.google.com/share/7a1ea86b598c) where I gave Gemini the full essay as text and we went paragraph by paragraph. Gemini would proactively provide context and anytime I was confused, I would ask questions. At first the responses were pretty vanilla. But after a few iterations and provocations we were able to stabilize on the spicy-yet-true. After our very long multi-week conversation, I annotated the original text with summaries. I thought this struck a good balance of maintaining the integrity of the original while making it more approachable.

I must say I learned so much more from this AI-enhanced reading process than I would have without it, and believe strongly in the potential for AI as a reading partner, especially for dense and difficult texts. Looking ahead, what better application of superintelligence is there than education?

You can [read the annotated version of Meltdown here](https://dmvaldman.github.io/LandAndExpand/).
